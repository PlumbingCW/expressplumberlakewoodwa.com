Gas Water Heater Problems in Lakewood, WA

If you smell gas near your water heater, leave the building now and call 911 or Puget Sound Energy from outside. Do not use a phone, light switch or garage-door opener inside, and do not go back to look. We are the second call, not the first.

If there is no gas smell, most gas water heater trouble shows up as no hot water, a pilot that keeps going out, rumbling, a leak or soot. This page covers what you can look at safely, what the symptoms usually mean, and which ones need a licensed plumber.

By the Lakewood Express Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

Read this first

The gas-smell rule

  1. Leave the building immediately with everyone and every pet.
  2. Do not flip switches, use a phone or open a garage door inside.
  3. From a safe distance outside, call 911 or PSE's emergency line at 1-888-225-5773.
  4. Stay out until the utility or fire crew says the building is clear.

Treat a rotten-egg smell as gas, even if you think it comes from the drain or the tank water. Sorting out the source is not a job for the person standing in the room.

What you can safely check from a distance

With no gas smell, a few looks cost nothing and help the plumber. Check whether other fixtures lack hot water, which separates a heater fault from a single clogged tap. Read the temperature dial on the control, and see whether it was bumped to low or vacation mode.

Look at the floor around the tank and the pipe that comes off the relief valve. Dampness at the base, rust streaks or a puddle in the pan tell us what we are dealing with. Take a picture before you call.

That is the end of the homeowner list. Do not remove the burner cover, relight a pilot, adjust a gas valve or touch the flue, because mistakes there can cause carbon monoxide or a gas leak.

Signs that mean call a licensed plumber

Pilot goes out again and again

A pilot that will not stay lit often points to a worn thermocouple or a draft problem. The repair and the relight are both for a licensed plumber.

Yellow, orange or lifting burner flame

A healthy flame is mostly blue. A lazy yellow or orange flame, or one that lifts off the burner, suggests poor combustion and needs a trained eye.

Soot, scorch marks or rust at the vent

Black marks around the draft hood or a rusty vent pipe mean exhaust is not leaving cleanly.

Rumbling or popping from the tank

Sediment on the tank bottom traps steam bubbles. It wastes gas and shortens tank life.

Water at the base of the tank

A leaking tank cannot be patched. Shut off the cold water supply to the heater if it is safe, and call.

Venting and carbon monoxide, without the jargon

A gas water heater burns fuel and has to push exhaust out of the house. If the vent is disconnected, blocked, rusted through or sloped wrong, exhaust can spill back into the room. That is called backdrafting, and it is one of the reasons a carbon monoxide alarm matters near any gas appliance.

Carbon monoxide has no smell. If an alarm sounds, or if several people have headaches and nausea that ease when they leave the house, get everyone outside and call 911.

A plumber inspecting a heater looks at draft, vent slope and joints as part of the visit. We do not suggest any do-it-yourself draft test.

Repair or replace?

Age and tank condition decide it. A heater with a worn thermocouple or a bad gas control valve is often worth fixing if the tank itself is sound. A tank that leaks from the shell is finished, because there is no repair for a corroded steel shell.

If the cost of the repair is close to what a new unit would cost, replacement usually wins. Our water heater repair visits start with a diagnosis, and we describe both routes before you decide.

When replacement is the answer, water heater replacement includes new connections, the relief valve discharge piping and a check of the venting.

Gas heaters in Lakewood: PSE, permits and the strap rule

Puget Sound Energy is the exclusive natural gas provider in Lakewood. PSE's guidance is to leave the building immediately and call PSE or 911 from a safe place, and to avoid anything that can spark. That order matters because the first minutes decide the outcome, not the plumber's arrival.

State plumbing code requires water heaters to be strapped against horizontal movement, with straps in the upper and lower thirds of the tank and the lower strap at least 4 inches above the controls. In an earthquake, an unstrapped tank can tip and tear its gas connector, so look at your straps now.

The city's older building FAQ also says a permit is required to install or replace a water heater. Confirm the current rule with the city permit center before a replacement.
