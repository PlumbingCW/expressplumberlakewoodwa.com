Water Bill Jumped? Find Out Why Before You Call Anyone

The bill came and the number looks wrong. Before you assume the worst, you can narrow down the cause in about an hour with a flashlight, a pen and a bottle of food coloring.

Most surprise bills trace to a running toilet, a sprinkler zone stuck on or a hidden leak in the line to the house. A meter test sorts these out.

By the Lakewood Express Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

The meter test, step by step

  1. 1

    Stop all water use

    Turn off faucets, the dishwasher, the washer and the ice maker. Make sure no one flushes.

  2. 2

    Open the meter box

    Lift the lid carefully and wipe the dial clean. Watch for insects or debris.

  3. 3

    Read the dial

    Find the low-flow indicator, a small triangle or dial that spins at the slightest flow. Note the reading.

  4. 4

    Wait and read again

    Wait 30 minutes without using water. If the number advanced or the indicator moved, water is leaking.

A moving indicator with everything off confirms a leak somewhere past the meter. If it holds still, the leak is not constant and you should look at the other usual suspects below.

Toilets lose the most water for the least noise

A toilet with a worn flapper can leak hundreds of gallons a month without making a sound. Put a few drops of food coloring into the tank, do not flush, and wait 15 minutes. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is not sealing.

A new flapper is a cheap part that most homeowners can swap. If the color stays put but the toilet still hisses, the fill valve may be at fault, and our toilet repair crew can handle either.

Sprinklers and hoses

  • A zone valve stuck partly open can run water into the lawn for days
  • A broken sprinkler head leaves a soggy patch or a geyser
  • A hose bib that drips adds up over a month
  • An automatic timer set after a power outage may run extra cycles

Turn the irrigation off at the controller and rerun the meter test. If the dial stops, the irrigation system is where the water went.

Reading the result

What a meter test tells you
What you seeLikely causeNext step
Indicator moves, everything offLeak in a line, fixture or toiletDye test, then call for detection
Indicator stops after irrigation is offSprinkler leakCheck zones and heads
Indicator never moves, bill is still highPast use, a fixture that leaks only when used, or a billing issueReview the bill and ask the utility
Meter spins even with the main valve closed at the houseLeak between meter and houseCall a plumber

That last row is the one that surprises people. A buried line between the meter and the building can leak for months without any wet spot showing, and it is a water service line repair matter.

When the meter might be the problem

Meters can misread, though it is rare. If your meter test shows no movement, your fixtures are all dry and the bill is still far above normal, ask your water utility to check the meter and review the reading history.

Photograph the dial on the day you test it. A dated picture gives the utility something concrete to compare with its own record. We do not speak for any utility on billing or adjustments, so ask yours what options exist.

Lakewood Water District: what you are responsible for

The customer's responsibility in the Lakewood Water District starts at the customer side of the water meter, so pipes from the meter to the building are yours to maintain and repair. Check your bill for your provider, because Parkland Water District and Summit Water and Supply Company serve parts of the area as well.

If a leak is gushing, the district offers 24-hour emergency shut-off, and emergency water shutoffs have no fee. Use your own main valve first if you can reach it, and call the district if you cannot.
