What You Actually Pay For in Leak Detection

Leak detection costs money because the leak you cannot see is the hard one. You are paying for the tools and the skill to find water inside a wall, under a slab or in a buried line without tearing everything open.

The price depends on where the pipe runs, how many methods it takes to confirm the spot and whether access is open.

By the Lakewood Express Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

What the detection fee covers

The fee is for locating the leak, not for fixing it. A good detection visit ends with a marked spot or a clear statement of where the leak is and how far along the line it sits.

  • 1
    Isolating the systemShutting sections of the plumbing off to see which part is losing pressure.
  • 2
    Choosing the methodMatching the tool to the pipe: listening, thermal imaging, camera or gas.
  • 3
    PinpointingMarking the exact spot so a repair opens one small area instead of a large one.
  • 4
    ReportingTelling you what was found, what was ruled out and what repair comes next.

Methods and when each one fits

Common leak detection methods
MethodWhat it doesBest for
Pressure testHolds water pressure on a section and watches for a dropConfirming a leak exists in a line
Acoustic listeningPicks up the sound of escaping water through ground or floorPressurized lines under slabs or yards
Thermal imagingShows temperature differences behind walls and floorsHot water lines and wet building materials
Moisture meterReads dampness in drywall, wood or flooringMapping the size of the wet area
Pipe cameraLooks inside drain linesDrain and sewer leaks

Few jobs need all of these. Often two methods agree, and that is when the plumber marks the spot.

What moves the price of a leak detection visit

We give a clear price before work starts, after talking through what you have seen. Hidden leaks do not come in a standard size, so the number follows the job.

  • Where the pipe runsA wall leak with open access is quicker than a line buried under a driveway or slab.
  • How many methods it takesTwo methods to confirm a spot means more time on site.
  • Access and obstructionsFurniture, tile, landscaping or a locked crawlspace adds time.
  • Size of the propertyLonger runs of pipe take longer to isolate and test.
  • Time of the callAfter-hours visits are staffed around the clock and priced before anyone arrives.

Many homeowners policies cover water damage from a sudden leak but not the cost of finding or repairing the pipe itself. Some include limited coverage for tear-out. Read your policy and ask your insurer before work begins.

Checks you can do before you call

  1. Turn off every faucet and appliance, then read your water meter.
  2. Wait 30 minutes without using water and read it again.
  3. Add a few drops of food coloring to each toilet tank and wait without flushing.
  4. Look for warm spots on the floor, musty smells or paint that is bubbling.

If the meter moves with everything off, you have a leak and the next question is where. If it does not move, the issue is likely a fixture, an irrigation valve or something else on the usage side. Our leak detection team can narrow it from there.

Leaks in Lakewood: where the meter divides the problem

In Lakewood Water District, the customer's responsibility starts at the customer side of the water meter. Pipes from the meter to the building are the homeowner's to maintain and repair, while the shutoff valve at the meter is utility-owned. If you are not sure who serves your address, check your bill, since Parkland Water District and Summit Water and Supply Company also serve parts of the area.

That helps you decide where to look. A leak on the street side of the meter is a utility matter, and a leak between the meter and the house is yours. A buried leak there often points to the water service line, which is its own kind of repair.

Source type: Lakewood Water District customer information.

What happens after the leak is found

Locating the leak is half the job. The repair may be a small patch on a copper line, a section of pipe replaced, or a full line replaced if the pipe is worn out in several places.

Whether opening the wall or the ground requires a permit depends on the work. The city's building FAQ says a permit is required before repairing or replacing a plumbing system, so confirm with the city permit center before work starts.
