What Goes Into a Main Sewer Line Cleaning Quote

Ask three companies about cleaning a main sewer line and you can hear three very different numbers. The gap rarely means someone is padding the bill. It usually means each company priced a different job.

No honest quote exists without knowing where the cleanout is, how far the blockage sits, what is causing it and what time you need the work done. Here is how each of those moves the price.

By the Lakewood Express Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

Five things that build the quote

Cleanout access

A working cleanout lets the cable go straight in. Without one, a plumber may have to pull a toilet or work from a roof vent, which adds labor.

Distance to the blockage

A clog near the house is a short cable run. One far out toward the street takes more cable, more time and sometimes a bigger machine.

What is blocking the line

Paper and soft buildup open quickly. Tree roots and hardened grease call for cutting heads or hydro jetting.

Camera work

A camera pass shows the cause and the pipe condition. It is a separate step, and many jobs are better for it.

Time of the call

Nights, weekends and holidays carry after-hours staffing. A clear price should be stated before anyone comes out.

Cleanout access: the cheapest feature on the property

A cleanout is a capped pipe, usually near the foundation or in the yard, that gives direct entry to the sewer line. With one in good shape, a cable machine sets up in minutes. When the cap is buried, painted shut or missing altogether, the first part of the visit is just finding a way in.

If you do not have a cleanout at all, adding one is worth asking about. It turns every future clog into a faster, simpler job and gives a camera a clean path into the line.

Cable, cutter or jet: matching the tool to the clog

Cleaning methods for a main sewer line
MethodGood forDrawback
Cable with a spade or auger headSoft clogs, paper, a quick restoration of flowLeaves pipe wall residue
Cable with cutting bladesRoot intrusion in the lineRoots regrow if the cause remains
Hydro jettingGrease, scale and heavy buildupNeeds a sound pipe and a cleanout

The right tool depends on what the first pass meets. Roots call for hydro jetting or cutters, and a quote that assumes a simple cable job can change once the plumber feels what is in the pipe.

Why the camera is worth pricing in

Clearing a clog without looking inside is like repainting a wall you have not checked for a leak. The line may be cracked, offset at a joint, or sagging into a belly that holds water.

A sewer camera inspection records the pipe so you can see the root entry points, the low spots and the condition of every joint. If the clog keeps returning, that footage is how you decide between more cleaning and a repair, and it keeps you from paying for the same visit twice.

Questions to ask before you agree to a price

  1. Does the quote include a camera pass, or is that extra?
  2. What method will you use, and why that one?
  3. What happens if the line is longer than expected or roots are found?
  4. Is the after-hours rate already included in the number you gave me?
  5. Will I see the camera footage if there is a problem?

A plumber who answers these plainly is pricing the job in front of them. Vague answers usually mean the number will move once the work starts.

Where insurance fits in

Most homeowners policies treat a clog as maintenance and do not pay to clean the line. Sudden damage from a sewage backup can be covered under optional coverage, so read your policy and photograph any damage before cleanup begins.

  • Maintenance versus damageClearing the line is typically your cost. Water damage from a backup may fall under a separate endorsement.
  • Recurring clogsIf the line backs up often, a repair may cost less in the long run than repeated cleaning.

A clear price before work starts lets you compare any quote against what your policy may cover.

Lakewood sewer lines: what you own and the permit path

Pierce County Public Works and Utilities provides sewer to almost all of Lakewood, and the county says the homeowner is responsible for the sewer line from the home to the public lines. A clog in that stretch is yours to clear, while one in the public main is not. That is why a camera and a clear report matter before you pay for anything.

If cleaning leads to a repair, the county's side sewer permit process comes into play. You apply through the county's online permitting system, the county reviews it, issues the permit, and then county Planning and Public Works inspects the work. Confirm the steps with the county before any digging starts.
