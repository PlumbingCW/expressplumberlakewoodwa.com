Can You Replace a Water Heater Yourself in Lakewood, WA?

Yes, some homeowners can replace an electric tank water heater themselves, and many shouldn't. The job is mostly shutting things off, draining, disconnecting and reconnecting, but a few steps carry real consequences if they go wrong.

A gas unit is a different story. If yours burns gas, the combustion and venting side of the job is where we'd tell you to stop and call a licensed plumber.

By the Lakewood Express Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

Who can reasonably do this and who should not

A handy person with a helper, a free afternoon and a like-for-like electric tank swap is the realistic DIY candidate. The new heater goes in the same spot, uses the same connections and needs no changes to venting or gas piping.

The job at a high level

This is an overview, not a manual. Read the instructions that come with your specific heater and follow them over anything written here.

  1. 1

    Shut off power and water

    At the breaker for an electric unit, and at the cold supply valve on top of the tank. Open a hot tap upstairs so the tank can drain.

  2. 2

    Drain the tank

    Attach a hose to the drain valve at the bottom and run it to a floor drain or outside. Expect hot water, so wait until it has cooled.

  3. 3

    Disconnect and remove

    Undo the supply lines and the relief valve piping, then take the old tank out. A full tank can weigh several hundred pounds, so drain it first and use a hand truck.

  4. 4

    Set, connect and refill

    Place the new tank, reconnect with new flexible lines, fill it fully with a hot tap open, and only then restore power.

That last order matters. Switching an electric element on while the tank is empty burns it out within seconds.

Where DIY swaps go wrong

Gas and combustion

A gas heater needs correct venting and a leak-tight gas connection. A mistake can leak gas or push exhaust into the house. This is the line where we say call a pro.

Relief valve piping

State code says the relief valve discharge piping may not have isolation valves and may not discharge into the heater pan. A shortcut here defeats a safety device.

Seismic strapping

Washington's code amendments require heaters to be strapped against horizontal movement, with straps in the upper and lower thirds of the tank. The lower strap sits at least 4 inches above the controls.

Old fittings that break

Corroded shutoffs and brittle unions can snap when you turn them. Suddenly you have a plumbing repair on top of a water heater job.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building first. Call PSE or 911 from outside, and do not use switches or your phone inside.

Relief valve and strapping: the two details people skip

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the part that opens when the tank gets dangerously hot or pressurized. The pipe running from it should point downward and end where hot water can't hit anyone. Never cap it, plug it or add a shutoff.

Strapping is the other detail. A tank that tips in an earthquake can tear its own gas or water connections loose. Two metal straps anchored to the framing are cheap compared with that outcome, so plan them into the job rather than adding them later.

What Lakewood homeowners should check first

The city's building FAQ says a permit is required for installing or replacing a water heater, with an inspection scheduled about a day ahead. That FAQ is an older document, so confirm the current process with the city permit center before you buy anything. Skipping the permit can come back when you sell the home.

Anyone doing plumbing work for hire in Washington must be certified by the state Department of Labor and Industries. That rule covers paid work, and the L&I online verify tool lets you check a plumber's certification before you hire. Ask for it, because a heater swap by an uncertified installer leaves you holding the risk.

What drives the cost of a swap

A DIY swap saves labor, but it isn't free of cost. You still pay for the heater, fittings, straps, a permit and possibly a haul-away. A professional quote is built from the same pieces plus the plumber's time.

  • Fuel type and sizeGas, electric and tankless units price very differently, and larger tanks cost more.
  • Venting and gas workChanges to flue or gas piping add parts and labor.
  • Code itemsStraps, relief valve piping and a pan are part of a proper install.
  • AccessA heater in a tight attic or crawlspace takes more handling.
  • Permit and haul-awayPermit handling and removal of the old tank are separate line items.

Ask any installer to list these items so you can compare quotes fairly. If your heater is under a manufacturer's coverage, read the terms before touching it.
