Fixing a Single-Handle Kitchen Faucet That Drips or Leaks

Your faucet drips at the spout every few seconds, or a ring of water gathers around the base each time you use it. Those two symptoms point to different parts, and guessing wrong costs a weekend.

A single-handle kitchen faucet fails in a handful of predictable ways, and most are fixed with a cartridge, a set of O-rings or a cleaned aerator.

By the Lakewood Express Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

Start here: read the leak

Drip from the spout when off

The cartridge or its seals no longer shut off flow completely. It is the most common single-handle fault.

Water at the base of the spout

Worn O-rings around the spout body let water seep out with every use.

Water under the handle

The cartridge seal or its retaining nut has loosened or worn.

Weak or splitting flow

Mineral grit in the aerator is often the cause, not the faucet itself.

Wet cabinet below

A supply line, a shutoff valve or a failing faucet body. Check the connections with a dry cloth.

Shut off the water before anything else

Look under the sink for two small valves, one for hot and one for cold. Turn each clockwise until it stops. Then open the faucet to drain the lines and confirm the flow has stopped.

If a valve spins freely or will not close, stop there. A stuck shutoff valve is a plumbing job in itself, and forcing it can snap the stem and leave you without a way to stop the water.

Replacing the cartridge

The cartridge is the cylinder inside the faucet body that controls flow and temperature. Over time, its seals harden and the faucet stops shutting off cleanly.

  1. 1

    Remove the handle

    Pry off the cap, loosen the set screw with a hex key, and lift the handle away.

  2. 2

    Pull the cartridge

    Remove the retaining nut or clip, note the cartridge orientation, and pull it straight out.

  3. 3

    Match the part

    Take the old cartridge to a supply store so you buy the exact model and brand.

  4. 4

    Reassemble and test

    Seat the new cartridge in the same direction, tighten the nut, and open the shutoff valves slowly.

Photograph each step as you go. A cartridge installed rotated the wrong way gives you hot water on the cold side.

O-rings and the aerator

When water gathers at the base of a swivel spout, the O-rings on the spout stem are the usual cause. Lift the spout off, slide the old rings out, and roll fresh ones into the grooves with a thin film of plumber's silicone grease. Match the size exactly, since a ring that is slightly too thick will bind the swing.

For weak flow, unscrew the aerator at the spout tip and rinse out the sand and flakes that collect on the screen. Soaking it in vinegar loosens mineral crusting. If the aerator is clean and flow is still weak, the problem is behind the faucet, in the supply line or shutoff valve.

Repair or replace: a simple test

When to repair and when to replace a single-handle faucet
SituationBetter choice
Drip only, faucet otherwise soundReplace the cartridge
Base leak with a working handleReplace the O-rings
Corroded body or cracked spoutReplace the faucet
Repeat repairs within a yearReplace the faucet
Parts no longer made for the modelReplace the faucet

Replacement also makes sense when you are changing the sink or countertop. If the supply lines or shutoff valves are old and stiff, replacing them at the same time costs little extra labor. Our faucet repair page covers what we handle.

Flush after faucet work in older Lakewood homes

The median home in Lakewood was built in 1974, and Lakewood Water District advises that homes built before 1985 may have lead pipes or solder. The district recommends flushing water that has sat for six hours or more, and running 6 to 8 ounces to flush brass faucets.

That matters right after a repair. Opening the plumbing can stir up debris and old solder particles, so run the cold tap for a bit before you drink from it. If you are unsure what your pipes are made of, ask the water district.

Source type: Lakewood Water District customer guidance and Census housing figures.

When to call a plumber

  • The shutoff valves will not close fully
  • Corrosion at the supply line connections
  • A leak that continues after a new cartridge
  • A faucet mounted on stone where the holes are fixed
  • Water damage in the cabinet below
