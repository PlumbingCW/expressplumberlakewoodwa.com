The first ten minutes: shut it off, drain it, stay clear

Water moves fast once a pipe lets go, so speed matters more than neatness. Work through these in order, and stop if any step looks unsafe.

  1. Close the main shut-off valve, usually where the line enters the house. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, call the water district for emergency shut-off at the meter.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to let trapped water drain, then open one upstairs to break the vacuum.
  3. If the water heater is near the flood and the tank is not under water, turn off its power or gas supply only if you can reach it safely.
  4. Stay away from outlets, appliances and the electrical panel while anything is wet. Call us from a dry spot.

Lakewood Water District offers 24-hour emergency shut-off, and emergency shutoffs have no fee. The valve at the meter is utility-owned, so ask the district to operate it rather than forcing it. Anything on your side of the meter is yours to repair.

Why pipes split here: the cold snap and the soil

Water expands about nine percent when it turns to ice, and that pressure splits pipe walls. Lakewood is mild on paper, but the nearest weather station recorded 21 days a year with a low at or below 32 degrees on average. A winter with a few of those nights, plus wind through an unheated crawlspace or garage, is enough to freeze a line.

The area has seen stretches that proved the point. Broken pipes marked the cold spell of 1985, and heavy snow hit Pierce County in the winter of 2008 to 2009, in 2012 and again in 2019. Arctic air returned in January of 2024. Burst pipe calls tend to arrive when the thaw begins and water finally flows through the split.

Buried lines are protected by depth. The city's design standards use a minimum frost depth of 12 inches, so a service line laid shallower than that, or exposed where it enters a building, is the one to worry about. Insulate exposed pipes and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights.

Cracked, split or pinhole: what the damage tells us

A burst or split pipe

A long split usually runs along the pipe and comes from freezing or a pressure spike. Water sprays or pours, and the section has to be cut out and replaced. This is the case where the main comes off first.

A cracked or broken pipe

Cracks show up at fittings, bends and spots where a pipe has been bumped, pulled or stressed by settling. They may weep rather than spray. A cracked or broken pipe can hide behind drywall for days, and the call often starts with a stained wall instead of a flood.

A pipe leak from corrosion

Pinholes and weeping joints are repairable when they are isolated. When they appear in several places, the pipe is usually wearing out, and patching one leak only moves the failure. Every leaking pipe is an urgent call, because water damage grows by the hour, and we treat it that way.

Your plumber identifies the material on arrival. We do not assume what is inside the walls of any particular home.

Copper repair is the most common call in this group. A clean freeze split in accessible copper is often a straightforward fix, while hidden copper in a finished wall adds drywall work.

Slip couplings and section swaps, or repiping?

A slip coupling slides over the cut ends of a pipe and joins them without needing the line to be pulled apart, which makes it handy in tight spaces. A section swap replaces a few feet with new pipe. Both fix the failure in front of us.

The harder question is whether this is the only weak spot. If the pipe is in good shape and a freeze caused the damage, a local repair is usually the sensible answer. If the line shows corrosion, repeated pinholes or brittle walls, we will tell you plainly and explain when whole-house repiping becomes the better value than another call-out.

Supply valves, drain pipes and other breaks

Burst or broken supply valve

The little shut-off valve under a sink or behind a toilet can crack, seize or leak at the stem. Close the main, then we replace the valve and the supply line together. A faucet repair visit often covers the fixture side.

Drain pipe repair

A cracked drain pipe leaks wastewater, not clean water, so the smell and the damp patch are your clues. Shut off water use in that part of the house and call. If the break is in the buried sewer line, a camera and a sewer line repair estimate come next.

Document the damage before you clean up

Insurers want evidence, and water damage disappears quickly once cleanup starts. Take wide photos of the room, close-ups of the pipe and the wet material, and a short video if water is still moving. Keep any failed piece of pipe in a bag.

Most homeowners policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe but may not cover the pipe repair itself. Call your insurer, ask what they require, and keep receipts for any dry-out work.

What drives the price of a burst pipe repair

After the water is off, we give a clear price before work starts. The number depends on what is broken and how hard it is to reach.

  • Pipe material and sizeA short length of copper or PEX is a smaller job than a long run of larger-diameter pipe.
  • AccessA split in an open basement is quick. A split behind a tiled shower wall includes opening and closing the wall.
  • How much pipe is badOne clean split is a section swap. Brittle or corroded pipe may need a bigger repair.
  • Location of the lineExposed pipe in a crawlspace is simpler than a buried line that needs excavation.
  • Time of the callAfter-hours dispatch is available, and the price is stated before we begin.
