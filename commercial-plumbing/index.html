Backflow testing: what the rule says

A backflow assembly sits on a pipe to stop used or contaminated water from flowing backward into the public supply. Washington rule WAC 246-290-490 requires assemblies to be tested every year by a certified backflow assembly tester. Irrigation systems that add chemicals are classed as high hazard.

The Lakewood Water District runs an annual backflow testing program for customers who need annual assembly testing, using a contracted tester. Ask the district whether your account is in that program before you book anyone.

  • Restaurants: carbonated beverage machines, dishwashers and boilers can all need protection.
  • Shops: irrigation on landscaped frontages is the usual assembly.
  • Offices: fire sprinkler lines and landscape irrigation.
  • Multifamily: irrigation and boiler feed lines.

A failed test normally means the assembly needs repair or replacement. That is plumbing work we do, and we do not do it without telling you the price first. The certified tester must hold current certification, so ask to see it.

Grease traps and why floor drains back up

A grease trap, or interceptor, is a tank that slows wastewater so fats, oils and grease rise and stay behind. When the trap is full, grease passes through to the sewer line, hardens on the pipe wall and narrows it. The first sign is often a floor drain in the kitchen that gurgles or fills.

Cleaning means pumping out the contents, scraping the walls and baffles, and checking the inlet and outlet. How often depends on the kitchen: a fryer-heavy menu loads a trap much faster than a sandwich counter.

We do not set an interval for you, because it depends on your volume, trap size and what your sewer utility requires. A simple log with dates, amounts removed and who did it helps in two ways. It shows when the trap fills faster than expected, and it gives an inspector a record.

Most of Lakewood's sewer service comes from Pierce County Public Works and Utilities. Ask them what is required for your kitchen. If drains keep backing up even after the trap is clean, the line needs a drain cleaning visit or hydro jetting.

Tracing a leak in a commercial building

Water loss in a larger building can hide in a ceiling, under a slab or in a riser. We start with the simple checks: meter reading with all fixtures off, pressure behavior and visible signs. Tracing equipment is then used where the evidence points.

Be aware that some commercial leaks turn out to be a tenant's fixture, a pipe owned by someone else, or a line that needs a specialist. We tell you honestly when that is the case, and our leak detection page explains the methods.

Cross-connection control

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) addresses cross-connection control, and Washington requires certain backflow assemblies to be tested by a certified tester. Which devices on your property need an annual test depends on the hazard and on the water utility's rules.

Ask your water utility which of your assemblies need testing and by when. We do not claim to test assemblies for you here, so tell the dispatcher if the problem involves one.
