When a clogged drain is an emergency

Drain cleaning means clearing a blockage out of a sink, tub, floor drain or sewer line so water flows freely again. It turns into an emergency when water has nowhere to go. If wastewater is rising in a tub, gurgling in a toilet or pooling around a floor drain, call us now and we will dispatch at any hour of the day or night, weekends and holidays included.

While you wait, stop using water anywhere in the house. Every flush, shower or load of laundry sends more water toward a line that is already full. Keep children and pets away from standing wastewater, and move rugs or boxes off the floor around the drain.

Water rises in a second fixture

Run the washer and the tub gurgles or fills. That points to a blockage downstream of both, usually in the main line.

A toilet will not flush and a sink will not drain

One stopped fixture is usually a local clog. Two or three at once means the problem sits further down the line.

Sewage smell with slow drains

Gas and wastewater push back up when a line cannot carry flow away, and that smell is a sign to stop using water.

Water at a floor drain or cleanout

Wastewater showing at the lowest opening in the house means the line is backed up completely.

Why drains clog in Lakewood homes

Housing age sets the baseline. The median home in Lakewood was built in 1974, and roughly 38 percent of homes went up between 1960 and 1979, with another 23 percent before 1960, according to Census survey figures for 2019 to 2023. Drain lines in houses that old have had decades to collect scale, grease and roots, and some have sagged or cracked at the joints.

That does not mean your line is failing. It means a clog in an older home deserves a proper look instead of a quick pass with a cable. A camera tells us whether we are dealing with a one-time blockage or a pipe that will keep catching debris.

Tree roots are the other local factor. Pierce County gets about 42 inches of rain a year at the nearest weather station, and wet ground keeps roots hunting for the moisture and nutrients inside a sewer line. Where mature trees stand close to a property line, roots are a common reason a sewer line clogs again after it has been cleared.

A little more than half of Lakewood households rent, so many calls start with a tenant or a property manager. If you rent, tell your landlord first when it is safe to wait, and call us directly when water is rising and you cannot reach anyone.

Clog types we clear, and how we approach each

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen lines clog with cooking grease that cools into a paste, coffee grounds, rice and food that slips past the strainer. The sludge builds inside the pipe wall, so water slows for weeks before it stops. A cable usually opens the line, and when grease has coated the full run we follow with a jetter to clean the pipe wall.

If the sink shares a line with a garbage disposal or dishwasher, we check both. A jammed disposal and a clogged drain often show up as the same complaint, and our garbage disposal repair team handles the unit itself.

Bathroom sinks, tubs and showers

Hair and soap scum bind into a mat just below the drain opening. Pulling the stopper or strainer clears some of it, and a small cable gets the rest. When a tub drains slowly even after cleaning, the vent or the trap arm may be the real problem, and we trace it rather than clearing the same clog every few months.

Floor drains and laundry lines

Basement and garage floor drains carry lint, sediment and sometimes the overflow from a failing main. Laundry standpipes overflow when lint and soap build up in a trap that is too small for the washer's pump. We clear the line and check that the drain slopes properly to the main.

Main sewer lines

A main line clog backs up the lowest fixtures first. We clear it with a larger cable or with hydro jetting when roots or heavy buildup are involved, then run a camera to see what caused it. If the camera finds a broken or collapsed section, the next step is a sewer line repair estimate with a clear price before any digging starts.

We name the method before we start and explain why. If a cable will do the job, you will not be sold a jetting visit. If the line keeps clogging, a sewer camera inspection shows what is happening inside the pipe so the fix matches the cause.

What affects the cost of drain cleaning

We give you a clear price before work starts, after we have seen the drain or talked through the symptoms. Drain work is not one flat number because the same complaint can come from very different problems.

  • Where the clog sitsA fixture trap is a short job. A main line blockage 60 feet from the cleanout takes longer and more equipment.
  • What is causing itSoft buildup clears quickly. Roots and hardened grease need heavier tools or jetting.
  • Access to the lineA working cleanout speeds the job up. Without one, we may need to pull a toilet or work from the roof vent.
  • Camera workAdding a camera pass to find the cause is a separate step, and you can choose whether you want it.
  • Time of dayNights, weekends and holidays are staffed around the clock, and after-hours visits are priced before we come out.

Store-bought drain openers: what to know first

Usually fine to try

  • A plunger on a single sink or tub
  • Pulling the pop-up stopper to remove hair
  • Hot water flushed after you clean a kitchen strainer

Call a plumber instead

  • Two or more fixtures backing up together
  • Gurgling toilets or sewage smell
  • A clog that returns within weeks of clearing

Chemical drain openers can sit in a blocked pipe and make the job riskier. They can burn skin, damage some pipe materials, and they leave a hazard for the plumber who opens the trap afterward. If you have already poured one in, tell the technician on arrival.

Who is responsible for which part of the line

In Lakewood, sewer service is provided by Pierce County Public Works and Utilities. The county's guidance says the homeowner is responsible for the sewer line from the house to the public line. That means a blockage in your side sewer is yours to clear, even if the cause is a root growing in from a street tree.

If our camera shows the problem is in the public main, we tell you so and show you the footage. That keeps you from paying to repair a pipe you do not own.

Washington requires anyone doing plumbing work for hire to be certified through the state's Labor and Industries department. You can look up any plumber or contractor with the state's online verify tool before you hire them.
