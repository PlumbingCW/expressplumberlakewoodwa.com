Emergency Hydro Jetting in Lakewood, WA

Hydro jetting in Lakewood, WA means scouring the inside of a drain or sewer pipe with a high-pressure stream of water. It cleans the pipe wall itself, which a cable cannot do.

The drain that keeps clogging after snaking

Plenty of households have paid for a snaking visit, watched the water drain, and then seen the same gurgle return in three weeks. That pattern has a mechanical explanation. A cable head spins through the middle of the pipe and bores a hole through whatever is blocking it.

The grease, scale or root hair stuck to the pipe wall stays where it is. New debris catches on that rough coating, and the opening narrows again. Jetting is the step that removes the coating instead of just punching through it.

Clogs return within weeks

The line opens after a cable pass but slows again quickly. The wall is still coated.

Gurgling after laundry or showers

Air is being pushed through a narrowed pipe. The main line may be partly blocked.

Sewer odor with no visible leak

Sludge and trapped grease give off gas. Cleaning the wall often ends the smell.

Roots returned after cutting

Cut roots regrow from the stub. Jetting flushes the fine hairs a blade leaves behind.

If you are not sure whether your pattern fits, our drain cleaning team can start with a cable and tell you honestly when the result will not last.

Sewage coming up right now: do this while we are on the way

Backups are the reason most people call us for jetting at night. We dispatch for them on weekends and holidays too, and a plumber will confirm on the phone whether the first visit will be a cable, a jetter or a camera.

Do this

  • Stop every tap, flush and appliance that drains
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the low drain
  • Keep children and pets out of the wet area

Avoid this

  • Pouring chemical openers into a full line
  • Running the washer to see if it clears
  • Touching outlets or cords in standing water
  • Walking barefoot through backed-up water

How a jetter works inside the pipe

The equipment is simpler than it sounds. A truck-mounted or portable unit pumps water through a reinforced hose, and a nozzle on the end of the hose shoots jets backward and sideways. The backward jets pull the hose forward through the pipe, and the sideways jets scour the wall.

We feed the hose in through a cleanout whenever there is one. At the far end of the clog we pull the hose back slowly, so the nozzle washes the debris toward the opening or downstream to the main.

When we will not jet: why the camera often goes in first

High-pressure water in a pipe that has collapsed, split lengthwise or separated at a joint can wash soil into the line or open the crack wider. That is why a plumber who has not seen inside the pipe may ask for a camera look before jetting. The footage shows whether the wall is sound enough to take the pressure.

We adjust the pressure and the nozzle for the pipe in front of us. A fragile line gets a gentler head, and a line with a visible break gets repair advice instead of jetting. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what the recording shows and what you get to keep.

If the camera finds a collapsed run or a cracked section, jetting is off the table and the conversation turns to sewer line repair. We will say so plainly and show you the footage.

Older pipe and cast iron: what changes the plan

Lakewood's median home was built in 1974, and about 23 percent of homes predate 1960, according to Census survey figures for 2019 to 2023. We do not know what material your line is made of until we see it, and we will not guess. What age does tell us is that the line has had decades to collect scale and root hairs.

If your drain pipe is cast iron, the inside is often rough and may carry rust nodules and heavy scale. Jetting can clean those lines well, but a corroded wall can be thin in spots. We start at moderate pressure, watch what comes back, and stop if the water carries pieces of pipe wall.

The practical point is simple. Tell the plumber what you know about your pipe, and let the camera confirm it before any pressure goes in.

These are starting points. What the plumber finds on site decides the final choice.

Cabling costs less in labor and equipment, which is why we do not push jetting on a first-time clog. Jetting earns its place when the same line has already been snaked and the problem came back.

What shapes the price of a jetting visit

You get a clear price before work starts, after the plumber has seen the access point and heard the symptoms. Jetting is not one flat job, because lines differ a great deal.

  • Length and size of the lineA short kitchen branch is quick. A long main line to the street takes more hose and more time.
  • Type of blockageSoft grease washes off in one pass. Hard scale or root mass takes extra passes or a different head.
  • AccessAn open cleanout saves time. If we must remove a toilet to reach the line, expect more labor.
  • Camera workA before-or-after camera pass is a separate step, and you choose whether to add it.
  • Time of dayNight and weekend visits are priced and explained before the truck rolls.

Insurance usually treats a jetting visit as maintenance. Damage from a sudden sewer backup may be covered only if your policy has a backup endorsement, so read yours and photograph any damage before cleanup.

Hydro jetting questions

A cable bores a hole through a clog but leaves grease, scale and root hair on the pipe wall. That coating catches new debris, so the clog returns. Jetting cleans the wall itself, which is why it lasts longer on lines that have already been snaked.

Snaking uses a spinning cable to break through a blockage. Jetting uses high-pressure water from a nozzle that scours the whole inside of the pipe. Snaking suits a first-time clog, and jetting suits recurring ones.
