The problems that make DuPont homeowners call at night

No hot water

On an electric heater, check that the breaker has not tripped. On gas, do not relight the pilot yourself, just call. A tank that has gone cold overnight is often a failed element, thermostat or igniter.

Water at the base of the tank

A tank that leaks from the bottom cannot be repaired. Shut off the cold-water inlet, and we price a replacement.

Wet ceiling or damp wall

Shut the main valve and stay away from the wet area. We find whether the source is a supply line, a drain or a fixture above.

Slow or backing-up drain

One slow sink is a local clog. Gurgling in several fixtures points to the main line.

Our overnight line is staffed, so a leak found at 11 p.m. is not a leak that waits for Monday. Gas smell is the one case where you leave the house first and call PSE or 911 from outside before calling us.

Repair or replace: a quick test for a failing water heater

Water heater repair versus replacement decision guide
What you seeLikely causeUsual path
Lukewarm water, breaker fineWorn element or thermostatRepair, see water heater repair
Rumbling or popping noiseSediment on the tank bottomFlush, then judge the tank's age
Rusty hot water onlyCorroded tank interiorUsually replacement
Puddle at the baseFailed tankReplacement, see water heater replacement

If the tank is more than about a decade old, replacement often costs less over time than a string of repairs.

Washington enforces the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code, and state code requires heaters to be strapped. We confirm the strapping and the relief valve discharge piping on any replacement.

What the city's own water means for your pipes and fixtures

DuPont owns its water system, with five wells on Bell Hill and Hoffman Hill, chlorine-only treatment and more than 3,000 connections. The city's reports do not give a hardness figure, so test your tap with a kit, or ask the city for its annual water quality report.

If you see white crust on faucets or heavy sediment when you flush the heater, tell the plumber. It helps us decide whether a flush, a filter or a different fixture is the practical answer. Do not assume your pipes or your water are lead-free, and ask the city what it reports.

Old village, newer master-planned neighborhoods: same call, different house

DuPont started as a company town in 1906, and that Historic Village is part of the city today. Northwest Landing, a master-planned area approved by the council in 1989, means those homes are newer than the old village. Age alone does not tell us the pipe type, so we ask you to describe the house and then look.

That is useful to know when you call. A newer home usually points us to fixtures, water heaters and appliance connections, while an older one makes us look harder at valves and drains.

Permits and sewer questions to ask the city

The city has an online permit portal. We found no plumbing or water heater permit rule to quote, so ask the city before your job. The same goes for sewer: we are not stating which agency serves your address, so ask the city.

Anyone doing plumbing for hire must be certified by the state Department of Labor and Industries, and you can check any plumber with L&I's online lookup. For help with a drain, our drain cleaning team is on the same line.
