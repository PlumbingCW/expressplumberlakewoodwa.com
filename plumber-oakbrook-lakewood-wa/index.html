What Oakbrook is

Local reporting describes Oakbrook as an early planned community in Lakewood. The developer bought the land in 1963, the first homes went up in 1964 and most were finished by the late 1960s. The original plan covered about 923 acres and roughly 2,300 houses.

Oakbrook Elementary, Oakbrook Park and the Oakbrook Golf Club are the names residents use for the area, and the city lists an Oakbrook Neighborhood Association that meets at the school. The city does not publish an official boundary, so this page does not draw one.

What a 1960s build year does and does not tell you

A house built in the mid-1960s has had decades of repairs, remodels and water heater changes, so its plumbing can be a mix of eras. The year a house was built is only a starting point for a plumber. Looking at the pipe is what answers the question.

One piece of guidance does depend on the date. The water district says lead pipes or solder may be present in homes built before 1985, and it recommends flushing water that sat for six hours or more. Oakbrook's original houses fall before that cutoff. If lead is a concern, ask the district what testing it recommends.

  • Look at exposed pipe near the water heater and under sinks, where the material is visible.
  • Ask the seller or previous owner which parts of the plumbing were replaced and when.
  • Use a camera inspection to see the drain line, since it is buried and cannot be judged by sight.

Drains and the sewer line

Most of Lakewood's sewer service comes from Pierce County Public Works and Utilities, and the homeowner is responsible for the line between the house and the public sewer. Your bill or county record shows who serves your address.

A single slow sink is a job for drain cleaning. If toilets, tubs and floor drains all slow down together, the trouble is probably further down the line, and a camera pass shows where. Water that shows up in the wrong place is a question for leak detection.

Water heaters: straps and permits

Washington's state amendments to the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) require water heaters to be strapped against sliding, with straps in the upper and lower thirds of the tank and the lower strap at least 4 inches above the controls. Ask whether any heater work includes checking the straps.

The city's building FAQ also says a permit is required to install or replace a water heater, so confirm current steps with the city. Our water heater replacement page explains the work.
