Plumbing Installation in Lakewood, WA

Plumbing installation is the work of putting new supply lines, drains, vents and fixtures into a building, whether that is a single sink or the whole system for an addition.

This page walks through how installs are staged, what to settle before the walls close and how to get the work set up well.

  • Remodels and additions

Two stages: rough-in and finish

Most installs happen in two visits that are weeks apart. The first happens while walls and floors are open, and the second after drywall, tile and cabinets are in.

Rough-in versus finish plumbing
StageWhat happensInspection
Rough-inDrain, waste, vent and supply pipes are set inside walls and floorsPipes stay visible until inspected
Pressure checkThe system is tested so leaks show before the walls closeOften checked at the same time
FinishToilets, faucets, sinks, tubs and appliance connections are setFinal inspection if required

The city's building FAQ says inspections are scheduled about 24 hours ahead. Confirm timing with the permit center.

Older Lakewood homes often need a little extra to bring new fixtures into old walls. The median home dates to 1974, and a cut-in to an existing line may reveal a corroded valve or fitting that needs replacing at the same time.

Remodels, additions and ADUs

A remodel that moves a sink or toilet means new drain slope and venting. Drains need a steady fall toward the main, and every fixture needs a vent to move air so water does not siphon the trap.

An addition or accessory dwelling unit adds fixtures that must connect to the existing supply and the sewer. That means sizing the water line for the new load and finding where the new drain will tie in.

If the project involves new water heating, see our water heater installation page. Sewer connection questions sit with the county, and if the existing line is old, a camera inspection before you build can avoid a surprise.

Water filtration add-ons

A filter or softener should start with a question: what is in your water? Test your tap, or ask the Lakewood Water District for its annual water quality report. We do not state hardness for any address.

A filter under the sink for drinking water is a small install. A whole-house system needs space, a drain and sometimes a bypass. Choose the system for the problem the test finds, not for a sales pitch.

Gas lines are not part of this page

Gas line work belongs to a licensed professional and to Puget Sound Energy, which is the gas provider in Lakewood. We do not give gas steps here. If you smell gas, leave the building immediately, then call PSE or 911 from outside, before calling us.

Permits, code and checking your plumber

Washington enforces the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code with state amendments, effective statewide since March 15, 2024. The City of Lakewood says it is not a full-service permit agency, and some sewer, water, electrical and fire-safety work goes through outside agencies.

The city's building FAQ says a permit is required before installing, altering, repairing or replacing a plumbing system. Confirm with the city permit center what applies to your job.

Anyone doing plumbing work for hire in Washington must be certified by the state Department of Labor and Industries. You can use L&I's online verify tool to check a plumber's certification and a contractor's registration before you agree to anything.

What shapes installation pricing

You get a clear price before work starts. Installs are priced by scope, not by guesswork.

  • Scope and fixture countMore fixtures and longer runs take more pipe and time.
  • Wall and floor accessOpen framing is quicker than cutting into finished surfaces.
  • Distance to main linesA bathroom far from the supply or sewer needs more pipe.
  • Fixture choiceYour selections affect both materials and set-up time.
  • Permits and inspectionsSome work needs a permit and a visit from an inspector.
