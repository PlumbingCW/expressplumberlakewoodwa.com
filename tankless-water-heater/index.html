Tankless hot water that runs lukewarm, weak or cold

Most tankless complaints start as a symptom, not a part name. The water turns cold for a few seconds in the middle of a shower, pressure on the hot side drops, or the unit shows a code and stops. Each points to a short list of causes.

A cold stretch between two bursts of hot water is the cold-water sandwich. It comes from the unit shutting off and restarting, or from water already in the pipes cooling. Weak hot pressure with normal cold pressure usually points to scale in the heat exchanger or a clogged inlet filter.

Cold-water sandwich

Hot, then cold, then hot again. Short draws can fall below the flow the unit needs to fire.

Low hot water pressure

Cold taps are fine, hot taps are weak. Scale or a clogged filter restricts flow through the unit.

Water never gets hot enough

A failing sensor, a burner issue or heavy scale can limit output.

Error code on the display

Codes point to ignition, flame, exhaust, temperature or flow faults. The manual lists what they mean.

Maintenance

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Minerals in water build up on the heat exchanger, and a thin scale layer makes the unit work harder and slows flow. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit to dissolve that scale. Many manufacturers call for periodic flushing, and the manual gives the interval.

Most units have two isolation valves on the hot and cold lines and a service port on each. The plumber closes the isolation valves, connects hoses and a small pump, and circulates a descaling solution through the exchanger. The unit is then flushed with clean water and the inlet filter is cleaned.

The solution matters. Some homeowners use white vinegar, but many manufacturers specify an approved descaler or a particular method. Follow the manufacturer's manual, since the wrong product can damage seals. If your heater has no isolation valves, we can add them so future flushes are simple.

Error codes in general terms

A code is the unit telling you where it stopped. Ignition and flame codes often point to gas supply, a dirty flame sensor or a blocked vent. Exhaust and vent codes point to a blocked or damaged vent. Temperature and flow codes often point to scale, a clogged filter or a sensor issue.

Try the simple things first. Check that the unit has power and that the display shows the code. Do not open the burner compartment or touch a gas fitting. If a code returns after a reset, call us.

Installing a tankless heater

An install changes more than the heater. Gas lines may need upsizing, venting may change and the relief piping needs planning. Washington enforces the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code, and the city's building FAQ says a permit is required for installing or replacing a water heater. Confirm with the city permit center.

Our water heater installation page covers the permit, venting and licensing points for planned work.

If your tank has already failed, see water heater replacement. If hot water matters tonight, a like-for-like tank is often the quicker route, and the tankless change can wait for a planned visit.

When a tank is the better choice

A tankless unit fits when

  • You have the gas capacity or panel space
  • Usage is steady and overlapping draws are limited
  • You plan to stay long enough to recoup the cost
  • You will keep up with flushing

A tank is often the better choice when

  • The gas line or panel would need a major upgrade
  • You use small amounts of hot water at once
  • The vent route is hard to build
  • You want the simplest, lowest-upfront option

We say so when a tank fits better. See our water heater repair page if your tank can be saved.

Who should service it

Tankless units combine gas, combustion, electronics and water treatment, so service belongs with a licensed plumber. Some faults need manufacturer support for a sealed or electronic part, and we tell you if that is the case. Check any plumber with the state Labor and Industries online verify tool.

Venting and gas details follow the manual and the code

A tankless unit must be vented and supplied with gas exactly as the manufacturer's listed installation instructions describe, and the adopted codes back that up. Vent material, length and termination are not places to improvise.

Ask which vent material the plumber plans to use and whether the gas supply was checked against the unit's rated input. We do not install or repair gas lines, and gas work belongs to the right licensed trade.
