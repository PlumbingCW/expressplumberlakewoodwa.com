Water Heater Installation in Lakewood, WA

Water heater installation in Lakewood, WA covers setting a new tank or heater where there was none, moving one, or changing its fuel. This page is for planned work, and we schedule same-day appointments when available. If your tank has already failed and is leaking, see our emergency water heater replacement page instead.

Four reasons people plan an installation

  • 1
    New constructionA new build or addition needs a heater sized for the finished house, with the gas line, vent or circuit roughed in before drywall.
  • 2
    RelocationMoving a heater out of a hall closet or garage corner gives better access and sometimes shorter hot water runs.
  • 3
    Fuel switchChanging from electric to gas, or from gas to a heat pump, changes the vent, gas piping and wiring.
  • 4
    UpgradeGoing to a larger tank, higher efficiency or a tankless model before the old one fails.

Each starts with a visit to look at the space, the existing connections and how the household uses hot water. For a broader remodel or addition, our plumbing installation page covers the wider scope.

Gas or electric: what the fuel choice changes

Puget Sound Energy is the exclusive natural gas provider in Lakewood. If you want a gas heater, the first question is whether gas is already piped to your home and to the location you want. A gas heater also needs a vent path and enough combustion air.

Electricity depends on the neighborhood. It generally comes from Tacoma Power in the north, Lakeview Light & Power in the east and Puget Sound Energy in the west. Check your power bill, since the provider affects rates and whether the utility has any programs for efficient heaters.

Electrical circuit work is done by a licensed electrician, and gas piping is done by a licensed plumber.

Venting, in plain terms

A gas heater needs a way to carry exhaust out of the house, and the vent type depends on the model. Atmospheric models use a metal flue that rises to the roof. Power-vent and direct-vent models use fans and can exit through a side wall.

We check the vent run, its slope and condition and the clearances before installing. If your old flue is corroded or does not suit the new tank, we explain what has to change. We do not quote vent sizes here since they depend on the model you choose and its manual.

Seismic strapping and relief valve piping

Washington enforces the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code with state amendments. State code requires water heaters to be anchored or strapped against horizontal displacement. Straps go in the upper and lower thirds of the tank, and the lower strap sits at least 4 inches above the controls.

The relief valve discharge piping may not have isolation valves, and may not discharge into the heater pan. We plan the discharge route to meet that rule when we choose the tank location.

The expansion tank question

A thermal expansion tank is a small tank that absorbs the extra volume when water is heated in a closed system. It is a common topic during installs, so you may hear about it. Whether your home benefits depends on the plumbing setup, and the installer evaluates the system, including whether a check valve or pressure reducing valve is on the supply.

We do not claim it is required for every home. If your system calls for one, we explain why and include it in the price before work starts.

Permits and inspection

The city's building FAQ says a permit is required before installing or replacing a plumbing system and for installing or replacing a water heater. It also says inspection is scheduled about 24 hours ahead. Lakewood is not a full-service permit agency, so confirm with the city permit center how your job is handled.

That matters for planning. A new install in a remodel may involve other trades and other agencies, such as electrical permits handled outside the city. We tell you what permits the project involves before we begin.

New construction plumbing for the heater

In a new build or addition, the heater location is chosen early because the gas line, vent, drain pan, relief valve discharge and a floor drain or exit route need to be planned together. A tank in a finished closet with no drain is harder to protect from a leak. We coordinate with the general contractor and the electrician so the rough-in is ready.

Rentals and multifamily buildings follow the same rules. The city's 2024 comp plan says over half of its newest housing is multifamily. If you manage several units, call and we will go over a schedule. Neighbors in Fircrest and DuPont can book the same planned visits, though permit rules differ by town, so ask the city.

Checking your installer

Washington requires anyone doing plumbing for hire to be certified through the state Department of Labor and Industries. Use the state's online Verify a Contractor, Tradesperson or Business tool to check registration, certification, workers comp and bond lawsuits. We are licensed and insured, and you should verify anyone you hire.

Where code shapes a new installation

Several details in a new install come from the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), 2021 edition, rather than from the plumber's preference. These include the relief valve discharge pipe, the clearances around the unit and the strapping that holds the tank upright.

If your home has a check valve or pressure reducing valve on the supply, the water system is closed. In that case the code calls for a thermal expansion tank, so ask whether one is needed.
